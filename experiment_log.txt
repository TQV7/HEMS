Experiment: Full-year baseline comparison
==============================

Setup:
- Dataset: UK household load + Agile tariff + solar generation (2024)
- Timestep: 30 minutes
- Battery round-trip efficiency: 0.95 charge / 0.95 discharge
- Initial SOC: 50% of capacity
- Terminal SOC floor enabled: 2 kWh
- Throughput penalty: 0.01 pence per kWh

Strategies compared:
1) Greedy heuristic scheduler
2) MILP optimisation scheduler (no battery export)
3) MILP optimisation scheduler (battery export allowed)

Results (annual electricity cost):

Greedy:
- Total cost ≈ £370

MILP (no battery export):
- Total cost ≈ £160.78
- Absolute savings ≈ £209.31
- Percentage savings ≈ 56.6%

MILP (battery export allowed):
- Total cost ≈ £98.81
- Absolute savings ≈ £271.28
- Percentage savings ≈ 73.3%

Observations:
- Optimisation significantly reduces electricity costs compared to heuristic control.
- Allowing battery-to-grid export enables price arbitrage behaviour.
- Export-enabled model achieves substantially higher savings but may be less realistic depending on tariff policy.
- Terminal SOC constraint prevents unrealistic end-of-day depletion behaviour.

==============================
Experiment: Seasonal performance breakdown (monthly aggregation)

Purpose:
- Analyse how optimisation benefits vary across the year.
- Understand the influence of solar generation and seasonal demand patterns.

Setup:
- Full-year simulation (Jan–Dec 2024)
- Half-hour resolution
- Battery capacity: 10 kWh
- Terminal SOC floor enabled: 2 kWh
- Throughput penalty: 0.01 p/kWh
- Compared strategies:
    1) Greedy heuristic
    2) MILP optimisation (no battery export)
    3) MILP optimisation (battery export allowed)

Key observations:

Winter months (Nov–Feb):
- Highest electricity costs observed.
- Optimisation provides significant savings due to limited solar generation.
- Battery primarily used for peak-price avoidance and load shifting.

Spring / Autumn (Mar, Sep, Oct):
- Moderate demand and moderate solar generation.
- Optimisation continues to reduce costs, though savings are lower than peak winter.
- Mixed behaviour with both price arbitrage and solar utilisation.

Summer months (Apr–Aug):
- Household becomes a net exporter of energy.
- Greedy strategy can already achieve negative net costs due to high solar output.
- Optimisation benefit decreases because solar dominates system economics.
- Export-allowed MILP shows strongest additional gains through arbitrage behaviour.

Overall insight:
- The economic value of battery optimisation is strongly seasonal.
- Optimisation provides the greatest relative benefit during low-solar, high-demand periods.
- In high-solar months, battery scheduling is less critical as export revenue offsets import costs.

--------------------------------------------------
Key interim conclusions
--------------------------------------------------
- Optimisation significantly reduces household electricity costs compared with heuristic control.
- Tariff volatility plays a major role in achievable savings.
- Export permissions materially affect optimisation performance.
- Terminal SOC constraints improve behavioural realism.
- Throughput penalties have modest economic impact but improve operational plausibility.

- Greedy scheduler tends to deplete battery early and cannot anticipate price spikes.
- MILP scheduler preserves energy for high-price periods.
- SOC floor constraint improves operational realism.
